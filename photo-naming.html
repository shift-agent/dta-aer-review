Naming your photos

One simple naming pattern means every photo you send files itself — onto the right product, in the right spot, on the website and in the rental system. No guessing, no manual sorting.

Why the name matters

Photos flow into the system automatically only when the filename tells the system two things: which product the photo shows, and what kind of photo it is. When the name carries both, the photo places itself the moment it arrives — nobody has to open it, recognize the linen, and file it by hand. When the name doesn’t, a person has to guess, and guesses are how photos end up on the wrong product or in no product at all.

The three kinds of photos — and how to name each

Every photo you’ll ever send is one of these three.

1

The 12 category images

The circle images on the home page — one per category. Name them category- plus the category name, with hyphens instead of spaces.

  • category-linens.jpg — the Linens circle
  • category-dance-floor.jpg — the Dance Floor circle
2

Product images — one per color

The main photo of the product itself (the fabric shot). Name it the product’s name including its color, all lowercase with hyphens, then _swatch. No sizes in the name — the size lives in the rental system.

  • madeline-damask-kiwi_swatch.jpg — the Madeline Damask in Kiwi
  • gold-chiavari-barstool_swatch.jpg — works for every product, not just linens
3

In-place images — the product at a real event

The styled “in action” photos. Same product-and-color name, then _inplace1, _inplace2… numbered when there’s more than one.

  • madeline-damask-kiwi_inplace1.jpg — Kiwi on a styled table
  • madeline-damask-kiwi_inplace2.jpg — a second event photo of the same linen

The color is part of the name The key rule

madeline-damask-kiwi_inplace1.jpg and madeline-damask-black_inplace1.jpg are different names — and that is exactly what lets the website show a different event photo for each color a visitor clicks. Pick Kiwi, see Kiwi on a real table; pick Black, see Black. If a photo isn’t named with its color, the site has no way to know which color chip it belongs to — the photo exists, but the website can’t use it.

Here’s the whole system on one product — your Madeline Damask in its three colors:

The five don’ts

Five habits to drop — each one breaks the automatic filing.

1

No spaces or capitals. Madeline Damask Kiwi.jpg ✗  →  madeline-damask-kiwi_swatch.jpg ✓

2

No “Copy of” or “(1)”. A second photo of the same kind gets the next number instead: …_inplace2.jpg

3

No sizes in the name. No 120" or 8' — sizes live in the rental system, and one photo serves every size of the same linen.

4

No iPhone HEIC files or huge originals if you can help it. Export as JPG. Big files still work — we shrink them — they’re just slower.

5

Don’t invent a name. Use the product’s name exactly as it appears on your sheet. If the photo’s product isn’t on the sheet, tell us instead of guessing.

If you’re ever unsure — send it anyway. A photo whose name we can’t match goes onto a “needs attention” list with our suggested fix, and we sort it out together. Nothing is ever lost or silently skipped — it just waits for a decision instead of appearing on the site under the wrong product.

The photos you’ve already sent

Everything already delivered gets renamed by us — we migrate the existing photos to this pattern as part of the build, and anything ambiguous comes back to you as a short question list, not homework. This convention is for new photos going forward: name them this way and they file themselves.
