01Where things stand

02The product ID concern — resolved

  • The worry: product references stopped matching, suggesting Current RMS was altering IDs.
  • The finding: Current RMS is not changing anything. We tested it directly against your live account.
  • The cause: the website's local copy was stale. Your team keeps adding products — we saw new items added the very day we looked.
  • After a refresh: 99.6% matched immediately. Two genuine exceptions remained, both already flagged for review.
What this means: no data loss and no rebuild. It also tells us the website needs to refresh itself from Current RMS automatically rather than relying on a manual export. That is a known, contained piece of work, and it removes this whole category of problem permanently.

07The path to the finish line

Every step names who it waits on. You = decides the schedule. Us = runs without blocking you.

  1. Waiting on you

    Agree what "finished" means

    A short written definition of the launch scope: which site or sites, which pages, what a customer can do, and what has to be true before we call it live. This is the single thing that has been missing, and it is what has caused this project to stall before.

  2. Waiting on you

    Settle the decisions in section 8

    One brand or two; browsing only or browsing plus quotes; how photography is handled; where your team edits; and the definition of done. We would expect this to take days, not weeks.

  3. Our work

    Publish what is already photographed

    Using the coverage table in section 5, we launch the categories that are ready — Flatware, Glassware, Crinkle, Dupioni, Chairs, Lighting and the linen ranges — rather than waiting for the whole catalog. Your site goes live with a real, browsable collection instead of sitting behind a photography backlog.

  4. Our work

    Extend product structuring to the rest of the catalog

    The largest build item, and the one that turns the remaining groups into properly browsable products.

  5. Our work

    Automatic refresh and improved quote capture

    The website keeps itself current from Current RMS, and enquiries arrive as itemised, priced opportunities rather than something to re-key.

  6. Together

    Photography, category by category

    We give you a shoot list ranked by what unblocks the most product, and each batch you shoot goes live as it arrives. This runs alongside everything above rather than holding it up.

  7. Our work

    Testing, tidy-up and launch

    Catalog accuracy, the quote journey end to end, mobile, speed, then the switch-over and redirects.

On dates. We have deliberately not put dates in this document. Committing to a timeline before scope is agreed is what caused difficulty here previously, and we are not going to repeat it. Once the decisions in section 8 are settled we will give you a phased plan with dates we intend to hold ourselves to, and a regular demonstration of working progress so you can see it moving.

On where your team edits. We have confirmed with your Current RMS account that the connection can write back, not only read. That means we have a genuine choice, so it is worth making deliberately rather than by accident.

Our recommendation: split it by the kind of information, not by the system.
  • Current RMS stays where the business is run. What exists, quantities, sizes, rates, availability. Nothing changes about how your team works day to day, and nothing can appear on the website that you cannot actually supply.
  • The website owns how things are presented. Photographs, descriptions, which categories are published, which brand a product belongs to, how sizes are grouped into one product. Current RMS has nowhere to put most of this, and the parts it does have — photographs and tags — are the parts that have not kept up.
  • Two things get written back to Current RMS so the benefit is not trapped in the website: brand markings, and photographs. Your own back-office views improve as a side effect of the website work.

What we would not recommend is editing the same field in both places. That is where these integrations go wrong: two people edit, nobody agrees who wins, and a scheduled sync quietly undoes someone's afternoon. One field, one home.
On running both brands from one catalog. This is achievable and the shared inventory approach is sound. One thing to be aware of: Current RMS currently holds no information about which brand a product belongs to — there is no existing marking we can read. So before both brands can run from one catalog, someone who knows the business needs to decide which products belong to Decor To Adore, which to Alabama Event Rentals, and which to both. Applying those markings afterwards is quick and we can automate it; the decisions themselves need your team.

Our recommendation: launch Decor To Adore first. It gets a working site in front of your customers soonest, proves the whole pipeline end to end, and means the Alabama Event Rentals rollout is a repeat of something already working rather than an experiment.
