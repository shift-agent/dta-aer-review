Photography plan

What photography already exists, why most of it cannot go live yet, and the order of work to change that — identification first, then shooting.

The real bottleneck is matching, not shooting

There are 254 product photographs already in hand — plus several hundred more event and venue images. The problem is not that the photography does not exist. It is that for 131 of those 254 we cannot tell which product in the rental system the photo shows, because the file name and the product name do not correspond. Until a photo is tied to a product id it cannot go on the site, no matter how good it is.

Recommended order

Identification comes first — it is far cheaper than shooting, and it is the only way to know what actually still needs a camera. Shooting follows, smallest categories first, so whole categories finish and go live rather than every one sitting half-done.

1

Identify the 131 photos we already hold

Someone who knows the inventory goes through the unmatched photos and says what each one is. They are already sorted and waiting in the media library, so this is a review job rather than a hunt. Roughly half are linens; the rest are spread across Table & Chairs, Bar Rentals, Decor and Stages — all categories currently showing nothing on the site. This costs no studio time and may well close a large part of the gap on its own.

How to hand photos over

There is now one shared folder that is the source of truth for product photography. A correctly named photo placed there appears on the site — there is no separate upload step, and nothing needs to be sent to us.

1. The folder

The shared Drive folder Website 2026 / _MEDIA LIBRARY now holds every product photo we have, already sorted. 1-READY contains the photos that are correctly named and live-ready, in a subfolder per website category. The other three folders are the ones that need your input — see below.

2. The file name

Every photo is named {product-id}--{color}--{role}--{number}.jpg — for example 3428--kiwi--lead--01.jpg. The product id is the item’s id in the rental system, the color is lowercase with dashes instead of spaces, and the role is lead for a straight product photo or inplace for a styled shot at an event.

The double dashes are what let the site read the name automatically, so they matter.

3. If you don’t know the product id

Put the photo in 3-UNMATCHED with a plain descriptive name and we will resolve it. It is much better to leave it for us than to guess an id — a wrong id puts the photo on the wrong product.

4. Shooting guidance

For consistency across the catalog: one photo per color option for linens, since color is what the customer is choosing. For chairs, tables, lighting and tableware, one clean photo per product is enough. Straight-on, even lighting, uncluttered background — the site crops to a square, so leave a little room around the product.

The home page and the inventory page use different categories

The home page presents twelve categories. The inventory browse page is built on a different set of six (Linens, Chairs, Tables, Tableware, Lighting, Structures). They are not two views of the same thing — they group the catalog differently, so a visitor moving from one to the other sees the collection reorganized underneath them. The twelve are the ones we understand to be correct; we need that confirmed so the inventory page can be rebuilt to match, and so the home page cards can link into it.

Two categories have nothing behind them

Pedestals / Columns appears on the home page but has no matching products in the rental system at all — either it is stored under another name, or the category should come off the page. Separately, Drapery (97 options) exists in the rental system but does not belong to any of the twelve — it needs a home, most likely Decor or its own category.

Home page still shows placeholder text and a duplicated band

The category section appears twice on the live home page — the second copy is labelled “landscape crop (compare)”, a review comparison that was never removed. Both copies carry the placeholder line “A short framing line that orients the cards below.” Neither should ship; the duplicate needs deleting and the real copy writing.

Product grouping needs a decision

Related products are not grouping together on the site — for example the four Madeline Damask colors each appear as their own separate product instead of one product with four colors to choose from. The rental system has no field that says which products belong to the same family, and the product spreadsheet does not have one either. We can either add that column to the spreadsheet on your side, or build and maintain the grouping ourselves. This is the main thing making the linen listing feel disorganized.
